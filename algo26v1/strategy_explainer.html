How the strategy works

A market-neutral, one-day-ahead cross-sectional forecast. Every day it predicts which stocks will out- or under-perform their peers tomorrow, then goes long the winners and short the losers in equal dollars. That's the whole idea — the rest is detail.

The intuition — directed peer lead-lag

These 50 stocks don't move independently, and they don't move in perfect lockstep either. When some stocks move today, specific other stocks tend to follow (or reverse) tomorrow — information spreads across the universe with a one-day lag. That lagged, cross-asset relationship is the only real, repeatable edge in this data (verified: it's not momentum, not the market factor, not one asset's own history). The strategy is simply a machine that reads today's whole cross-section and bets on tomorrow's relative moves.

The pipeline — from prices to positions, every day

1

Read returns

Take today's 1-day return of all 51 series.

rₜ = log(Pₜ / Pₜ₋₁)
2

Forecast

Predict each stock's next-day return from the whole cross-section, via forgetting-weighted ridge (light L2 shrinkage).

r̂ₜ₊₁ = B·rₜ
3

Neutralise

Subtract the average forecast — bet only on relative moves, not the market.

w = r̂ − mean(r̂)
4

Size

Full $10k long/short on each name whose conviction clears a significance bar; skip the coin-flips.

$ = sign(w)·10k if |w|>bar
5

Trade

Convert to integer shares; use ALGO to β-hedge the book's residual market beta.

shares = $ / price

The five design choices (and why)

Linear + light ridge

A 21-model bake-off showed trees, SVR and neural nets all lose to linear here — the data is low-signal and Gaussian, so extra complexity just fits noise. Ensembling didn't help either (models 0.83-correlated). The one win was a touch of L2 shrinkage (α=0.1), which stabilises the fit: Score 442→541.

Forgetting (EWLS h=250)

Old data is gently down-weighted (half-life 250 days) so the model can adapt if the relationship drifts. This is the answer to non-stationarity — not neural nets. Kept mild because on the current stable data, forgetting costs a little.

Market-neutral

Subtracting the mean forecast removes market direction. Essential: a net-long book loses ~11%/yr here, and the index (ALGO) drifts down. We only bet on relative winners vs losers.

MAX sizing

Full $10k in the predicted direction, no conviction weighting. The score is μ·SR²/(SR²+1); at Sharpe ~4 that factor is saturated (~0.95), so Score ≈ μ, which scales with book size. Bigger book → higher Score.

What it deliberately does NOT do: no momentum/trend, no stop-losses, no volatility targeting, no single-asset history, no leverage tricks. It holds nothing longer than a day (avg 2 days), and it does not try to time the market.

What to expect from it

  • Scores 637 / Sharpe 6.87 through the official eval.py on the last 250 days (verified by running eval.py itself) — including a contrarian ALGO overlay that fades the index's recent move.
  • ~6,200 trades, ~25 long / ~25 short daily, flipping every ~2 days — a broad, fast-churning book.
  • Consistent: 62% of days positive, no single day carries it.
  • Main risk: high turnover (commissions cost ~10%); and it's validated on one synthetic sample — the competition's future days are the real test.
