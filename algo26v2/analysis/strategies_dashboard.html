02

Score is not Sharpe — and recency matters

Weak signals earn their seat. Because score = mean·SR²/(SR²+1), the Sharpe penalty is gentle (SR 3 → ×0.90, SR 2 → ×0.80). A signal that only shows signs of edge still lifts score if it adds mean-PnL on idle names/capacity faster than it costs Sharpe. That's why the broad and kitchen-sink books (lower Sharpe, more signals) stay competitive on score.
Front-loaded ≠ durable. Strategy 3's 538 comes mostly from the older half of the window (first-125 = 731, last-125 = 269). Strategy 9 scores 509 but is balanced and strong on recent data (525) — the regime grading will actually see. Prefer the book that scores on the last-125, not the one that peaked early.

Why ~540 is the full-window wall

Score is bounded by max gross ($600k, from the $10k/$100k position limits) × the signal's return-per-dollar. The two trade off:

Pair quality# pairsSharpePnL per $Mavg gross

Tight pairs are ~70% more efficient but deploy less; loose pairs deploy more at lower efficiency. deployment × efficiency conserves at mean-PnL ≈ $550/day. Fancier signals — z-proportional sizing, Kalman, mean-variance/Avellaneda-Lee portfolios — all lowered return-per-dollar in testing. 800 appears only on a favourable sub-window (first-125 = 731), not robustly on the full window.
