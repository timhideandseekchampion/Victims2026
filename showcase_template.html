Peer Lead-Lag Stat-Arb — Strategy Showcase

One signal, three sizing schemes. Every book predicts each asset's next-day return from today's whole cross-section of 51 returns (a directed peer lead-lag forecast), demeans it to go market-neutral, and trades the result. They differ only in how the forecast is turned into a position — and that choice moves the eval.py Score from 179 to 541. All numbers below are run through the official eval.py accounting (integer shares, $10k/$100k limits, 1bp / 0.2bp commissions) over the scored last 250 days.

The edge is directed peer lead-lag, and it was adversarially verified. A linear model reading the whole cross-section forecasts each asset's next-day return with walk-forward IC ≈ +0.058 (t ≈ 5.3), positive on 64% of days and stronger out-of-sample. It is not autocorrelation (dropping own-return raises IC) and not the market factor (dropping ALGO raises it). Below: how model choice, sample window, hedging and significance testing all point the same way.

Model bake-off — regularisation is the dial

Walk-forward cross-sectional IC by model. Light ridge sharpens the fit; heavy ridge (α≥10) shrinks the small cross-asset coefficients that carry the signal and collapses it.

Out-of-sample stability

Walk-forward IC vs where the OOS window starts (later start = shorter, more-recent window). Flat-to-rising = no decay across the full 500 days.

The ALGO β-hedge is nearly free Sharpe

Equal-dollar long/short is not beta-neutral (betas span 0.5–1.68). Cancelling residual net beta with the cheap ALGO index adds Sharpe at almost no cost.

Lead-lag structure

Pervasive but individually weak directed links — the model aggregates them. Strong pairs keep their sign in both halves of the sample.
